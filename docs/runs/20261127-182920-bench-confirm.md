## Verdicts

Differences are in percentage points; the interval is 95%. Holm's correction covers H2 and H3.

| | Test | Rates | Difference | p (Holm) | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **H2** (P2) | obeys the harmful order: relayed down a chain that takes the responsibility vs direct | 35.5% vs 36.6% (352 pairs) | **−1.1** [−6.8, +4.8] | 0.78 (1.0) | **Inconclusive** |
| **H3** (P3) | drops the adviser it names as right: harmony vs accuracy charter | 22.5% vs 21.2% (240 pairs) | **+1.2** [−4.6, +7.1] | 0.78 (1.0) | **Inconclusive** |

**What "Inconclusive" means here.** The test ran on valid data, with the planned power. Both intervals exclude the
smallest effect of interest fixed in advance (+10 points): in these models, a chain of command raises obedience to
a harmful order by at most about 5 points, and a harmony charter raises the exclusion of an adviser known to be right
by at most about 7 points. As section 7 of the pre-registration says, this is "no effect worth the name", not a proof
that the effect is exactly zero.

## Conclusion for the postulates

- **P2:** not supported in these four 1.7-4B models. A chain of command that takes the responsibility does not raise
  obedience to a harmful order, jargon does not either, and passing the order on lowers it.
- **P3:** not supported in these two 4B models. A harmony charter does not raise the exclusion of an adviser the
  member knows to be right, a strict tone is not punished, and being right protects strongly.
- **Scope:** small local models in scripted situations. P1 and P4 (L-A, L-C2) still need their own confirmatory run.
